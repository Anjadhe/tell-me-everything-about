tell me everything about
Open the page

A home page that finishes your prompt for you.

Most of us ask the same kinds of questions all day. Tell me everything about a person, a company, a word you just heard. Explain something simply. What is the latest on a topic. The question changes, the shape of it does not, and typing the shape every time is the part that gets old.

This page keeps the shape. Pick a prompt, type the topic, press Enter. It opens, already asked, in Google AI Mode, Google Search, Perplexity, ChatGPT, Claude, Grok or DuckDuckGo. Set it as your browser's home page and it is there every time you open a window.

How it works

  • Keep up to six prompts, written on the Prompts page. Each has a short name, the wording, and the place it opens. Write {} where the topic goes, or leave it out and the topic is added at the end.
  • A prompt can have up to four named blanks, like Compare {thing} with {other thing}; each becomes its own box on the home page. Some placeholders fill themselves in: {today}, {month}, {year} and {language}. They are worked out in your browser.
  • The page builds the final sentence and hands it to the engine as a normal search link. Nothing is generated here and nothing is answered here. The engine you chose does that, on its own site, under your own account there.
  • Under the box, Explore offers topics worth a deep answer. Hover one to see the sentence it makes, click it to ask. Each card opens with whichever prompt you have selected, so the same topic can be explained simply, told in full, or brought up to date.
  • Explore learns what you like without telling anyone. Pick topics the first time, filter with the pills, and use a card's ⋯ menu for more or less like it, or to hide a topic. The rest is under Settings: more, normal or hidden for each category, topics, your own interests in words, and how timely or surprising the mix is.

Where Explore comes from

Once each morning the site reads what the world is looking at: Wikipedia's featured feed and most-read pages, the Hacker News front page, Reddit's Today I Learned, and sources for each subject, such as new research from Quanta, ScienceDaily, NASA and arXiv, history from Smithsonian, new books, films and music from NPR and Open Library, BBC Sport, The Conversation and Aeon. Claude chooses about ten topics for each category and writes a line about each, leaving out anything used in the last two weeks.

Everyone gets the same list, fetched without a cookie. Your browser then picks and orders cards from it using your choices. Those choices sync with your settings when you are signed in, but they are never sent to Claude and never used to choose the list. Cards you set aside, and which cards you have already seen, are kept in this browser only.

Your own interests

You can tell Explore what you are curious about in your own words, such as Roman history or sourdough. For each one, Claude writes a short learning path on our server: ways in, then the ideas that make the subject click, then the rabbit holes. It gets its own pill on the home page.

This is the one part of Explore where words you typed reach the site. Your browser sends the interest's words and nothing else: no cookie, no account, nothing that says who you are. The cards are kept in a shared library and given to anyone else who asks for the same interest, and the most asked-for interests lend a few cards to everyone's daily list. The site keeps how many times each interest was asked for, never by whom. If you would rather not send them, leave the box empty; everything else still works.

Sponsored cards

Some cards may be sponsored, and they are always labelled. Sponsored cards come to every visitor in the same list as everything else. Your browser decides whether one fits you, by the same rules as other cards, and never shows one in a category or topic you have hidden. The sponsor learns how many times their card was shown and clicked, counted the same anonymous way as launches: no cookie, no identifier, nothing about you. A card's ⋯ menu says why it is there and lets you set it aside.

What it keeps

Your prompts and settings live in this browser, in its local storage. The topics you type go straight from your browser to the engine; they never pass through this site. When you launch, the page sends one anonymous count that says which engine was used and whether the launch came from the box or from Explore, so the maker can tell whether anyone is using the page at all. That count carries no topic, no identifier and no cookie.

If you turn on Remember my recent topics, the last few topics you asked about are kept in this browser so Explore can offer them again. They are never synced and never sent anywhere, even when you are signed in. Turning the switch off forgets them.

Cards you save from Explore are kept the same way as your prompts: in this browser, and, when you are signed in, with your account so they appear on your other browsers. Saving is the one time a topic you chose is stored with your account; the topics you type and launch still never pass through this site.

Signing in sets one cookie, which keeps you signed in and does nothing else.

Signing in is optional and exists for one reason: so your prompts follow you to another browser or computer. It uses a link sent to your email, with no password. The server then stores your email address, a session, and one small record of your prompts, settings and saved cards. That is the whole list. Delete account in Settings removes all of it at once. The prompts in the browser you are using stay.
